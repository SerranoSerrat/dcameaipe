3.1For applied researchers

Before running an interaction model, it's worth writing down in one sentence which comparison the research question actually calls for — groups as they are, or groups on equal footing. If the two answers could plausibly diverge (treatment is dose-like, and groups differ in typical dose), a linear specification is a modeling choice that deserves a justification, not a default.

3.2For reviewers and readers

A reported interaction coefficient is only as interpretable as the assumption behind it. When treatment distributions differ across the groups being compared, it's reasonable to ask an author which quantity — dCAME or AIPE — the headline number corresponds to, and whether the conclusion survives relaxing the linearity assumption.

3.3For policy-facing work

dCAME describes the world as it currently is: it's the right quantity for summarizing an observed disparity. AIPE describes a counterfactual world where exposure is equalized: it's the right quantity for predicting what happens under a policy that changes who gets how much treatment. Using the wrong one doesn't just introduce statistical bias — it can answer a different policy question than the one being asked.
